# Exploring the Nexus Between Fertility Rates and Geopolitical Risk with Intelligence Methods: A Multifaceted Analysis

**Authors:**  
Tzitiridou-Chatzopoulou, M. et al.

**Year:**  
2024

**Source:**  
Healthcare

**Dataset / Study Area:**  
Monthly birth records in Scotland from January 1998 to December 2022 combined with the Geopolitical Risk Index.

---

## Research Objective

This study investigates how geopolitical risk influences fertility behaviour and birth rate forecasts. The research examines whether political instability, conflict, and uncertainty can affect individuals' decisions regarding childbirth. The study also evaluates whether incorporating the **Geopolitical Risk Index** improves the predictive accuracy of birth rate forecasting models.

---

## Methodology

The study compares machine learning algorithms with classical statistical approaches to forecast birth rates.

Models evaluated include:

- **XGBoost** – a gradient boosting algorithm designed to optimise predictive performance and capture complex nonlinear relationships.
- **Tree-based machine learning models** – ensemble methods used to improve predictive accuracy.
- **Traditional statistical regression models** – used as a baseline comparison.

The analysis also introduces the **Geopolitical Risk Index** as an additional explanatory variable within forecasting models.

### Evaluation Approach

To ensure robust forecasting results, the study applies:

- **rolling estimation methods**
- **cross-validation techniques**

These approaches help evaluate model performance on unseen data and reduce the risk of overfitting.

---

## Data

The dataset combines:

- **Monthly birth records in Scotland (1998–2022)**
- **Geopolitical Risk Index values**

The Geopolitical Risk Index measures levels of global geopolitical uncertainty based on events such as:

- wars
- political instability
- international tensions
- terrorism.

The study examines whether fluctuations in geopolitical risk correlate with changes in birth and fertility rates.

---

## Key Findings

The results demonstrate several important insights:

- **Machine learning models effectively predict birth rates in Scotland.**
- **XGBoost and other tree-based algorithms produced strong forecasting performance.**
- Incorporating the **Geopolitical Risk Index significantly improved forecasting accuracy.**
- Geopolitical instability can influence fertility decisions, as individuals may delay or reconsider having children during periods of conflict or uncertainty.
- Disruptions to healthcare systems, such as reduced access to maternal care, may also affect birth outcomes.
- Nonlinear machine learning models outperform traditional linear regression approaches in forecasting birth rates.

---

## Conclusion

The study demonstrates that **machine learning approaches can improve birth rate forecasting while capturing complex relationships between demographic behaviour and external risk factors**.

The findings suggest that geopolitical uncertainty can influence fertility trends through:

- changes in social behaviour
- economic uncertainty
- disruptions to healthcare systems.

Accurate forecasting models incorporating geopolitical indicators may therefore provide better insights for policymakers when planning for future demographic changes.

The authors recommend that future research:

- expands the geographic scope beyond Scotland
- explores additional machine learning algorithms
- incorporates further explanatory variables such as economic indicators.
