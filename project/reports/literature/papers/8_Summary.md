_The recent decline in period fertility in England and Wales: Differences associated with family background and intergenerational educational mobility_

## Abstract

During 2010–20, period fertility in England and Wales fell to its lowest recorded level. The aim of this paper is to improve our understanding of the decline in period fertility in two dimensions: differentials by the education of a woman’s parents (family background) and by a woman’s education in relation to that of her parents (intergenerational educational mobility). The analysis finds a substantial decline in fertility in each education group, whether defined by a woman’s parents’ education alone or by a woman’s own education relative to her parents’ education. Considering parents’ and women’s own education together helps differentiate fertility further than analysing either generation’s education in isolation. Using these educational mobility groups more clearly shows a narrowing of TFR differentials over the decade, but timing differences persist.

## Summary of Conclusion

The study finds that the sharp fertility decline in England and Wales between 2010–2020 occurred across all education and family background groups, indicating that the decline was driven by broader societal factors rather than changes within specific social groups.

It shows that family background and educational mobility influence the timing of childbirth. Women with less educated parents tended to have children earlier, while upwardly mobile women delayed childbearing more.

“The substantial recent decline in period fertility was experienced irrespective of education group.”

The results also show that although fertility rate differences between education groups narrowed over time, timing differences in childbirth remain.

“Upwardly mobile women delayed their childbearing much more than downwardly mobile women.”

Overall, the study suggests that fertility decline reflects widespread postponement of childbearing across society, which helps explain the downward trends observed in birth-rate time series used in forecasting models.

## Objective

Investigates socioeconomic drivers of declining fertility, which helps interpret patterns in birth-rate time series.

## Methods Used

* Analysis of UK Household Longitudinal Study
* Statistical modelling of fertility behaviour
* Examination of family background and education mobility

## Best Performing Model

The study primarily uses statistical regression analysis rather than forecasting models.

## Key Outcomes

* Fertility decline varies across family background groups.
* Individuals experiencing upward educational mobility tend to delay fertility.
* Family socioeconomic context influences reproductive behaviour.

## Relevance to Our Project

Highlights micro-level factors influencing fertility, supporting interpretation of long-term birth trends.
