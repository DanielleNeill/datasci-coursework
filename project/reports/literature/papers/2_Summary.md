_Mathematical Modeling and Analysis on the Population Birth Rate Trends in Japan_

## Abstract

Japan faces a signifcant demographic challenge with far-reaching social, economic, and political ramifcations due to its declining birth rate and rapidly rising aging population. Addressing the intricate aspects of these serious emergent phenomena, this study has proposed a mathematical model using a system of ordinary diferential equations that has been observed analytically and numerically. As part of the analytical solution, we have verifed the model’s existence and uniqueness, investigated its stability at the equilibrium points, and explored its characteristics concerning diferent state variables. Besides, a sensitivity analysis has been conducted to identify the sensitive parameters based on the estimated parameters. Additionally, the Runge–Kutta fourth-order approach has been used to fnd the numerical solutions. Tis study’s fndings have been investigated using a newly proposed mathematical model to highlight the demand for a broad awareness campaign in Japan to address the possible efects of a declining birth rate on social structure and the country’s economy, as well as concerns about rising prices infuencing family planning decisions. To sustain population growth in Japan, the government should provide proper initiatives to boost the birth rate.

## Conclusion

Japan, a country with the world’s third-largest economy, is experiencing an unprecedented population catastrophe with signifcant social, economic, and political consequences due to a declining birthrate and a fast population. To study this burgeoning problem, the research has put out a mathematical model to depict the dynamics of birthrate in conjunction with population growth. Subsequently, both theoretical and numerical evaluations have been performed on this model. Using the positivity and boundedness theorems, we have concluded our theoretical analysis that our model has a unique solution and that it is biologically valid. Te future status of the Japanese population provides insights into whether the population is projected to grow or decline. According to several real-world relevant criteria, the frst equilibrium point E1 is unstable, whereas the second one, E2, is globally unstable but locally asymptotically stable. Among other qualitative studies, the proposed model does not expose any limit cycle. Afterward, we conducted several tests to determine the features, and one of them is for σ (the rate of providing incentives), we found that both (dP∗/ dσ) > 0 and (dB∗/ dσ) > 0. Again, a sensitivity analysis was performed using the values obtained from parameter estimation, which allowed for the identifcation of the parameters that show their sensitive nature. After that, numerical simulations were performed out, which revealed that if the trend of the population dropping continues, then Japan would be faced with a signifcant challenge by the year 2050 due to the lower presence of young people as a result of a reduction in the birth rate. Regarding this matter, as the workload increases, there is a gradual rise in price infation. Additionally, the growing reliance on technology leads to a more rapid decline in the birth rate. Furthermore, immigration can show a positive impact on the population rise in Japan. It is recommended to raise awareness about the decreasing number of babies born in Japan and provide substantial incentives from the government and other social development organizations. Additionally, eforts should be made to control price infation. Failure to address this ongoing decline in birth rate and population could pose an alarming risk to the survival of the country. Terefore, more incentives and awareness campaigns including price defation and reduced-load work may boost the birth rate of Japan. As part of future work, optimal control would be used to optimize the cost of incentives and arrange awareness programs.

## Objective

Develop a mathematical population model to analyse declining birth rates and demographic dynamics in Japan.

The paper highlights Japan’s demographic challenge, noting that the country’s birth rate is “much lower than the replacement level necessary to sustain a steady population.”

## Methodology

The study constructs a system of ordinary differential equations (ODEs) to model population dynamics.

Techniques used include:

* equilibrium analysis
* stability analysis
* parameter sensitivity analysis
* numerical simulation using the Runge–Kutta method

## Models Used

* Mathematical population model based on ODE systems
* Sensitivity analysis of model parameters

Unlike the other papers, this study focuses on demographic dynamics rather than forecasting accuracy.

## Key Findings

* Declining fertility rates are driven by economic and social factors.
* Population systems exhibit stable equilibrium under current demographic conditions.
* Policy interventions may be required to increase fertility.

The study also emphasises that demographic change has significant economic and social implications, particularly for labour markets and healthcare systems.
